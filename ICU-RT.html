STEPWISE ACID-BASE INTERPRETATION
VBG in critical care: VBG pH and PaCO₂ correlate reasonably with ABG in hemodynamically stable patients (pH offset ≈ 0.03–0.05, PvCO₂ ≈ PaCO₂ + 3–8 mmHg). PvO₂ reflects mixed venous saturation, not arterial oxygenation.

Auscultation Technique
\n\n
\n
STEPLADDER PATTERN
\n
\n

Systematic side-to-side comparison moving inferiorly \u2014 each step compares the same anatomical level bilaterally before descending. This detects asymmetry that top-to-bottom unilateral auscultation misses.

\n \n \n
\n
\n\n
\n
STANDARD SEQUENCE
\n
\n
\n
1
Full cycle \u2014 listen through a complete inspiration AND expiration at each location before moving. One breath minimum.
\n
2
Side-to-side comparison \u2014 assess same anatomical level bilaterally before moving inferiorly (stepladder). Asymmetry is the primary finding.
\n
3
Posterior first \u2014 posterior exam covers more lung (predominantly lower lobes, highest pathology yield) with fewer acoustic obstacles.
\n
4
Anterior \u2014 upper lobes, RML/Lingula. Note that upper lobes are only reliably accessed anteriorly (apices) and above the scapular spine posteriorly.
\n
5
Lateral axillary lines \u2014 critical for RML (right) and Lingula (left), which have no posterior surface. Often omitted and a significant blind spot.
\n
6
Arm crossing \u2014 ask patient to cross arms in front when auscultating upper posterior zones. This rotates scapulae laterally and opens acoustic access to posterior RUL/LUL segments.
\n
\n
\n
\n\n
\n
NORMAL SOUNDS BY LOCATION
\n
\n
\n
Tracheal
Above suprasternal notch. Very loud, high-pitched. Exp > Insp with audible gap.
\n
Bronchial
Manubrium sterni, above clavicles. Loud, high-pitched. Exp > Insp, same loudness, gap between phases. Abnormal if heard peripherally.
\n
Bronchovesicular
1st\u20132nd ICS parasternal; interscapular posteriorly. Medium pitch/loudness. Insp = Exp, no gap.
\n
Vesicular
Peripheral lung fields. Soft, low-pitched. Insp > Exp in duration. Exp softer. No gap. Normal everywhere except above locations.
\n

MANEUVERS FOR SPECIFIC FINDINGS
\n
\n
\n
\u2191
Egophony (E-to-A sign) \u2014 ask patient to say \"EEE.\" Over consolidated or compressed lung, this transmits as \"AYY.\" Indicates lung-tissue consolidation (pneumonia) or compressed lung above an effusion.
\n
\u2191
Whispered pectoriloquy \u2014 ask patient to whisper \"1-2-3.\" Normally inaudible or muffled peripherally. Distinctly audible whisper over consolidation indicates increased transmission through solid lung.
\n
\u2191
Bronchophony \u2014 ask patient to say \"99.\" Normally muffled peripherally. Loud, clear transmission suggests consolidation. Decreased or absent over effusion or pneumothorax.
\n
\u2191
Cough test for crackles \u2014 fine crackles that clear with coughing suggest secretions (not alveolar disease). Persistent crackles post-cough are more consistent with pulmonary edema, fibrosis, or pneumonia.
